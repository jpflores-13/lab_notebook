Citrate/iodixanol density-gradient nuclei prep for single-nucleus applications

Published

October 7, 2026

NoteOverview

CitraPrep isolates intact nuclei from snap-frozen tissue for downstream single-nucleus work. Tissue is dounce-homogenized in a citrate-based homogenization buffer (CitraHB), then nuclei are purified on an iodixanol step gradient in an ultracentrifuge (18 min run, ~1 hr total with load/accel/no-brake coast, 10,000×g, no brake) and collected from the gradient interface. RNase inhibitors (RNasin Ribonuclease Inhibitor + Protector RNase Inhibitor) go into every buffer, including the final nuclei wash / resuspension buffer. Spermine, spermidine, and dithiothreitol (DTT) are deliberately left out of that buffer because they interfere with downstream cDNA synthesis.

For small, soft tissue that doesn’t need a density gradient, see the SeaPrep protocol instead.

ImportantBefore starting
  • Use cell culture grade water for every “water” call in this protocol (stock buffers and 1000X fresh solutions alike), not general lab ddH₂O.
  • Make 1 M Tricine–Potassium Hydroxide (Tricine-KOH, pH 7.8) and 1 M Citric Acid stocks first; CitraHB and Diluent are both built from them.
  • Prepare CitraHB, Diluent, and Detergent stock buffers a day ahead; good for 1 month at 4°C.
  • Pre-chill the 7 mL glass dounce homogenizer on ice.
  • Hand-dounce only: no motorized homogenizer, it shears nuclei and causes blebbing.
  • Make 1 M Dithiothreitol (DTT), 0.15 M spermine, and 0.5 M spermidine (1000X stocks) fresh each time.
  • Make CitraHB Complete (stock + protease inhibitor tablet) fresh; add spermine/spermidine/DTT to bring it to 1X.
  • Make the nuclei wash / resuspension buffer while the gradient is running, with no spermine, spermidine, or DTT in this one.
  • Set the ultracentrifuge to 4°C and confirm the rotor/tubes are rated for no-brake deceleration.
  • Book more time than the spin itself. The 18 min is only the run; loading, vacuum draw-down, acceleration, and the unbraked coast to a stop routinely bring the whole ultracentrifuge step to around an hour.

TipWhy Tricine needs KOH and citric acid doesn’t

Tricine-KOH is the free-acid form of Tricine titrated to pH 7.8 with Potassium Hydroxide (KOH); the “-KOH” in the name refers to this titration, not a separate reagent added at the bench. Citric Acid stock is used as-is; both CitraHB and Diluent get their acidic character partly from it, so no pH adjustment is needed here.

Add RNase inhibitor to all buffers below at the final concentrations set in Configure experiment above. The defaults are 0.5 U/µL RNasin Ribonuclease Inhibitor (RNasin) and 0.5 U/µL Protector RNase Inhibitor (Protector), 1 U/µL total. RNasin and Protector don’t always ship at the same stock strength (commonly 40 U/µL or 20 U/µL); check your tubes, set all four values up top, and every table below rescales. Both are referred to by their short names (RNasin, Protector) throughout the rest of this document.

Working solutions: scaled to sample count

NoteMath audit: how every volume is calculated

CitraHB Complete — made in 10 mL increments because each aliquot takes one whole protease inhibitor tablet, so the total is rounded up to the next 10 mL:

\[V_{make} = \left\lceil \frac{v_{per\ sample} \times N_{samples} \times (1 + overage)}{10} \right\rceil \times 10 \qquad tablets = \frac{V_{make}}{10}\]

RNase inhibitors and the 1000X polyamine/DTT additives scale linearly with \(V_{make}\).

Nuclei wash / resuspension buffer — one pool covers the wash spin(s) and the final resuspension:

\[V_{buffer} = \left( v_{wash} \times n_{spins} + \frac{V_{final\ \mu L}}{1000} \right) \times N_{samples} \times (1 + overage)\]

Default is one wash spin of 10 mL plus a 200 µL final resuspension ≈ 10 mL/sample. Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA) is 1% w/v of \(V_{buffer}\); RNasin and Protector use \(V_{stock} = (C_{final}/C_{stock}) \times V_{buffer}\), each with its own stock strength. No spermine, spermidine, or DTT; these interfere with downstream cDNA synthesis.

Iodixanol step gradient — each of the three working solutions is diluted from base 50% iodixanol (itself 5 parts OptiPrep : 1 part Diluent, since OptiPrep ships at 60% and \(5/6 \times 60\% = 50\%\)) with CitraHB, using \(C_1V_1 = C_2V_2\):

\[V_{50\%\ needed} = V_{total} \times \frac{\%_{target}}{50\%} \qquad V_{CitraHB} = V_{total} - V_{50\%\ needed}\]

Per-sample totals: 15 mL of 50% (kept as-is), 4 mL of 40%, 7.5 mL of 30%; all scale with \(N_{samples} \times (1+overage)\). Polyamine/DTT additives scale as \(V_{total}/1000\); RNasin and Protector use the same \(C_{final}/C_{stock} \times V_{total}\) formula.

Iodixanol step gradient

WarningMake base 50% first, split it, then add additives to each tube separately

The 40% and 30% solutions are diluted from additive-free base 50% iodixanol, not from the finished 50% solution in the first table below. Work in this order:

  1. Make base 50% iodixanol with OptiPrep + Diluent only. No spermidine, spermine, DTT, RNasin, or Protector yet.
  2. Pull off the “50% iodixanol (above)” volumes listed in the 40% and 30% tables. That aliquot carries no additives with it.
  3. You now have three separate tubes (leftover 50%, 40% mix, 30% mix). Add spermidine, spermine, DTT, RNasin, and Protector to each tube, scaled to that tube’s own total volume.

Each tube is dosed once, on its own. The RNase inhibitor in the 40% and 30% tables is not a repeat of the 50%; the 50%-derived iodixanol in those tubes started out additive-free.

Protocol

1 · Tissue homogenization (~30 min)

WarningNo motorized homogenizer

Hand-dounce only; a rotor-stator or other motorized homogenizer shears nuclei and causes blebbing.

TipUnder-homogenize on purpose

A run that was deliberately under-homogenized gave intact nuclei with no blebbing at all. Stop douncing as soon as nuclei start releasing; you do not need a clear homogenate, and over-douncing loses more nuclei than it frees.

Two-round dounce: the ≈ 5 mL CitraHB Complete per sample is split 4 mL + 1 mL, and the 320 µL Detergent per sample is split 256 µL + 64 µL to match.

Step Action Per sample
a Pre-chill the 7 mL glass dounce homogenizer on ice. —
b Cut snap-frozen tissue into < 0.5 cm pieces with clean scissors in a chilled petri dish on ice. —
c Transfer the pieces to the dounce with 4 mL CitraHB Complete; rest 7 min on ice. 4 mL CitraHB Complete
d Dounce ~20× with the loose pestle; add 256 µL Detergent; dounce ~20× with the tight pestle. Under-homogenize (see tip); pull a drop to confirm nuclei are releasing. 256 µL Detergent
e Filter through a 40 µm strainer into a tube on ice; keep this filtrate. —
f Return the tissue chunks left on top of the strainer to the dounce with the remaining 1 mL CitraHB Complete + 64 µL Detergent; dounce again. 1 mL CitraHB Complete + 64 µL Detergent
g Filter through the same strainer and pool with the round-1 filtrate (≈ 5 mL/sample). —

2 · Iodixanol gradient centrifugation (~1 hr + spin)

WarningNo brake on the spin

This spin is run on an ultracentrifuge: 18 min at 10,000×g at 4°C with no brake. Braking disturbs the gradient interface and mixes the layers together, ruining nuclei recovery. Schedule the ultracentrifuge for 18 min run time, but budget closer to an hour for the step overall; loading/vacuum draw-down, acceleration, and the unbraked coast to a stop add substantial time beyond the programmed 18 min.

Step Action Per sample
a Add 50% iodixanol solution to the pooled homogenate filtrate and vortex to mix. 5 mL 50% iodixanol
b Underlay the sample with 30% then 40% iodixanol solutions. Per gradient table above
c Balance the conical tubes against each other on a scale, topping up the lighter tube(s) dropwise with cell culture grade water to within ~0.1 g; load balanced tubes opposite each other in the rotor. —
d Centrifuge 18 min at 10,000×g, 4°C, no brake. —
e Collect nuclei from the interface layer with a P1000 pipette; transfer to a new tube. —

TipHemocytometer counting method

CitraPrep nuclei yields are typically too low for a reliable Countess (automated) read, so this is a manual hemocytometer count every time; do not substitute the Countess.

  1. Filter your trypan blue stock through a 0.2 µm filter before use; unfiltered trypan blue carries particulates that get miscounted as nuclei, and this matters more at low nuclei numbers where a few false counts skew the result.
  2. Mix the nuclei suspension with filtered trypan blue at a 1:1 ratio (e.g. 10 µL suspension + 10 µL trypan blue, dilution factor = 2).
  3. Load 10 µL of the mix onto the hemocytometer; let it wick in by capillary action, no bubbles.
  4. Under the microscope, count nuclei in the 4 corner grid squares (16 small squares each). Use the standard hemocytometer counting rule: count nuclei touching the top and left borders of a square, but not the bottom or right, to avoid double-counting nuclei that sit on a line.
  5. Calculate concentration:

\[Nuclei/mL = \frac{\text{sum of nuclei in 4 corner squares}}{4} \times 2\ (\text{dilution factor}) \times 10^4\]

Divide by 1000 to convert to nuclei/µL for loading calculations.

## Working solutions: scaled to sample count

::: {.callout-note collapse="true"}
## Math audit: how every volume is calculated

**CitraHB Complete** — made in 10 mL increments because each aliquot takes one whole protease inhibitor tablet, so the total is rounded **up** to the next 10 mL:

$$V_{make} = \left\lceil \frac{v_{per\ sample} \times N_{samples} \times (1 + overage)}{10} \right\rceil \times 10 \qquad tablets = \frac{V_{make}}{10}$$

RNase inhibitors and the 1000X polyamine/DTT additives scale linearly with $V_{make}$.

**Nuclei wash / resuspension buffer** — one pool covers the wash spin(s) and the final resuspension:

$$V_{buffer} = \left( v_{wash} \times n_{spins} + \frac{V_{final\ \mu L}}{1000} \right) \times N_{samples} \times (1 + overage)$$

Default is one wash spin of 10 mL plus a 200 µL final resuspension ≈ 10 mL/sample. Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA) is 1% w/v of $V_{buffer}$; RNasin and Protector use $V_{stock} = (C_{final}/C_{stock}) \times V_{buffer}$, each with its own stock strength. No spermine, spermidine, or DTT; these interfere with downstream cDNA synthesis.

**Iodixanol step gradient** — each of the three working solutions is diluted from base 50% iodixanol (itself 5 parts OptiPrep : 1 part Diluent, since OptiPrep ships at 60% and $5/6 \times 60\% = 50\%$) with CitraHB, using $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$:

$$V_{50\%\ needed} = V_{total} \times \frac{\%_{target}}{50\%} \qquad V_{CitraHB} = V_{total} - V_{50\%\ needed}$$

Per-sample totals: 15 mL of 50% (kept as-is), 4 mL of 40%, 7.5 mL of 30%; all scale with $N_{samples} \times (1+overage)$. Polyamine/DTT additives scale as $V_{total}/1000$; RNasin and Protector use the same $C_{final}/C_{stock} \times V_{total}$ formula.

:::

1. Make base 50% iodixanol with **OptiPrep + Diluent only.** No spermidine, spermine, DTT, RNasin, or Protector yet.

## Protocol

### 1 · Tissue homogenization (~30 min)

::: {.callout-warning}
## No motorized homogenizer
Hand-dounce only; a rotor-stator or other motorized homogenizer shears nuclei and causes blebbing.
:::

::: {.callout-tip collapse="true"}
## Under-homogenize on purpose
A run that was deliberately under-homogenized gave intact nuclei with **no blebbing at all**. Stop douncing as soon as nuclei start releasing; you do not need a clear homogenate, and over-douncing loses more nuclei than it frees.
:::

Two-round dounce: the ≈ 5 mL CitraHB Complete per sample is split 4 mL + 1 mL, and the 320 µL Detergent per sample is split 256 µL + 64 µL to match.

| Step | Action | Per sample |
|------|--------|-----------|
| a | Pre-chill the 7 mL glass dounce homogenizer on ice. | — |
| b | Cut snap-frozen tissue into < 0.5 cm pieces with clean scissors in a chilled petri dish on ice. | — |
| c | Transfer the pieces to the dounce with **4 mL CitraHB Complete**; rest 7 min on ice. | 4 mL CitraHB Complete |
| d | Dounce ~20× with the loose pestle; add **256 µL Detergent**; dounce ~20× with the tight pestle. Under-homogenize (see tip); pull a drop to confirm nuclei are releasing. | 256 µL Detergent |
| e | Filter through a 40 µm strainer into a tube on ice; keep this filtrate. | — |
| f | Return the tissue chunks left on top of the strainer to the dounce with the **remaining 1 mL CitraHB Complete + 64 µL Detergent**; dounce again. | 1 mL CitraHB Complete + 64 µL Detergent |
| g | Filter through the same strainer and pool with the round-1 filtrate (≈ 5 mL/sample). | — |

### 2 · Iodixanol gradient centrifugation (~1 hr + spin)

::: {.callout-warning}
## No brake on the spin
This spin is run on an **ultracentrifuge**: **18 min at 10,000×g at 4°C with no brake**. Braking disturbs the gradient interface and mixes the layers together, ruining nuclei recovery. Schedule the ultracentrifuge for **18 min** run time, but budget closer to **an hour** for the step overall; loading/vacuum draw-down, acceleration, and the unbraked coast to a stop add substantial time beyond the programmed 18 min.
:::

| Step | Action | Per sample |
|------|--------|-----------|
| a | Add 50% iodixanol solution to the pooled homogenate filtrate and vortex to mix. | 5 mL 50% iodixanol |
| b | Underlay the sample with 30% then 40% iodixanol solutions. | Per gradient table above |
| c | Balance the conical tubes against each other on a scale, topping up the lighter tube(s) dropwise with cell culture grade water to within ~0.1 g; load balanced tubes opposite each other in the rotor. | — |
| d | Centrifuge 18 min at 10,000×g, 4°C, **no brake**. | — |
| e | Collect nuclei from the interface layer with a P1000 pipette; transfer to a new tube. | — |

::: {.callout-important}
## Checklist
- [ ] Gradient tubes weighed against each other and balanced to within ~0.1 g before spinning
- [ ] Balanced tubes loaded opposite each other in the rotor
- [ ] Ultracentrifuge confirmed at no-brake setting
- [ ] Interface layer collected cleanly, minimal carryover of gradient layers
:::

::: {.callout-tip}
## Hemocytometer counting method
CitraPrep nuclei yields are typically too low for a reliable Countess (automated) read, so this is a **manual hemocytometer count** every time; do not substitute the Countess.

a. Filter your trypan blue stock through a 0.2 µm filter before use; unfiltered trypan blue carries particulates that get miscounted as nuclei, and this matters more at low nuclei numbers where a few false counts skew the result.
b. Mix the nuclei suspension with filtered trypan blue at a **1:1** ratio (e.g. 10 µL suspension + 10 µL trypan blue, dilution factor = 2).
c. Load 10 µL of the mix onto the hemocytometer; let it wick in by capillary action, no bubbles.
d. Under the microscope, count nuclei in the 4 corner grid squares (16 small squares each). Use the standard hemocytometer counting rule: count nuclei touching the top and left borders of a square, but not the bottom or right, to avoid double-counting nuclei that sit on a line.
e. Calculate concentration:

$$Nuclei/mL = \frac{\text{sum of nuclei in 4 corner squares}}{4} \times 2\ (\text{dilution factor}) \times 10^4$$

Divide by 1000 to convert to nuclei/µL for loading calculations.
:::
