Gradient-free citrate nuclei prep for small, soft tissue

Published

September 9, 2026

NoteOverview

SeaPrep isolates nuclei from small, soft tissue (e.g. ganglia) for single-nucleus work. Tissue is homogenized in a citrate-based homogenization buffer (CitraHB) with a handheld homogenizer, then nuclei are pelleted and washed at low speed — no iodixanol gradient and no ultracentrifuge. RNase inhibitors go into the CitraHB Complete and the resuspension buffer. Spermine, spermidine, and dithiothreitol (DTT) are left out of the resuspension buffer because they interfere with downstream cDNA synthesis.

For firm or snap-frozen tissue that needs density-gradient purification, use the CitraPrep protocol instead.

ImportantBefore starting
  • Use cell culture grade water for every “water” call in this protocol.
  • Make 1 M Tricine–Potassium Hydroxide (Tricine-KOH, pH 7.8) and 1 M Citric Acid stocks first; CitraHB and the resuspension buffer are built from them.
  • Prepare CitraHB and Detergent stock buffers a day ahead; good for 1 month at 4°C.
  • Make 1 M Dithiothreitol (DTT), 0.15 M spermine, and 0.5 M spermidine (1000X stocks) fresh each time.
  • Make CitraHB Complete (stock + protease inhibitor tablet) fresh; add spermine/spermidine/DTT to bring it to 1X.
  • Make the resuspension buffer with no spermine, spermidine, or DTT in it.
  • A handheld/electric homogenizer is fine here — the tissue is soft.

Working solutions: scaled to sample count

NoteMath audit: how every volume is calculated

CitraHB Complete — made in 10 mL increments because each aliquot takes one whole protease inhibitor tablet, so the total is rounded up to the next 10 mL:

\[V_{make} = \left\lceil \frac{v_{per\ sample} \times N_{samples} \times (1 + overage)}{10} \right\rceil \times 10 \qquad tablets = \frac{V_{make}}{10}\]

Resuspension buffer — one pool covers the wash spin(s) and the final resuspension:

\[V_{buffer} = \left( v_{wash} \times n_{spins} + \frac{V_{final\ \mu L}}{1000} \right) \times N_{samples} \times (1 + overage)\]

BSA is 1% w/v of \(V_{buffer}\); SUPERasin and Protector use \(V_{stock} = (C_{final}/C_{stock}) \times V_{buffer}\), each with its own stock strength.

homog_rows = [
  {Action: `Homogenize the tissue (e.g. ganglia) in CitraHB Complete + Detergent in a round-bottom tube with a handheld homogenizer.`, "Per sample": `${fmt(chb_per_sample)} mL CitraHB Complete + ${fmt(detergent_uL, 0)} µL Detergent`},

  {Action: `Spin 10 min at 500×g, 4°C; discard the supernatant.${n_wash_spins > 1 ? ` Repeat the resuspend + spin so the nuclei are washed ${n_wash_spins}× in total.` : ""}`, "Per sample": n_wash_spins > 1 ? `${fmt(wash_per_spin)} mL per wash` : "—"},
  {Action: "Resuspend the final pellet; count on a hemocytometer.", "Per sample": `${fmt(final_resus_uL)} µL`},
  {Action: "Load for single cell.", "Per sample": "—"}
].map((r, i) => ({Step: i + 1, Action: r.Action, "Per sample": r["Per sample"]}))

Inputs.table(homog_rows, {layout: "auto"})
TipHemocytometer counting method

Nuclei yields are typically too low for a reliable Countess (automated) read, so this is a manual hemocytometer count every time.

  1. Filter your trypan blue stock through a 0.2 µm filter before use; unfiltered trypan blue carries particulates that get miscounted as nuclei, and this matters more at low nuclei numbers where a few false counts skew the result.
  2. Mix the nuclei suspension with filtered trypan blue at a 1:1 ratio (e.g. 10 µL suspension + 10 µL trypan blue, dilution factor = 2).
  3. Load 10 µL of the mix onto the hemocytometer; let it wick in by capillary action, no bubbles.
  4. Under the microscope, count nuclei in the 4 corner grid squares (16 small squares each). Count nuclei touching the top and left borders of a square, but not the bottom or right, to avoid double-counting nuclei that sit on a line.
  5. Calculate concentration:

\[Nuclei/mL = \frac{\text{sum of nuclei in 4 corner squares}}{4} \times 2\ (\text{dilution factor}) \times 10^4\]

Divide by 1000 to convert to nuclei/µL for loading calculations.

::: {.callout-tip}
## Hemocytometer counting method
Nuclei yields are typically too low for a reliable Countess (automated) read, so this is a **manual hemocytometer count** every time.

a. Filter your trypan blue stock through a 0.2 µm filter before use; unfiltered trypan blue carries particulates that get miscounted as nuclei, and this matters more at low nuclei numbers where a few false counts skew the result.
b. Mix the nuclei suspension with filtered trypan blue at a **1:1** ratio (e.g. 10 µL suspension + 10 µL trypan blue, dilution factor = 2).
c. Load 10 µL of the mix onto the hemocytometer; let it wick in by capillary action, no bubbles.
d. Under the microscope, count nuclei in the 4 corner grid squares (16 small squares each). Count nuclei touching the top and left borders of a square, but not the bottom or right, to avoid double-counting nuclei that sit on a line.
e. Calculate concentration:

$$Nuclei/mL = \frac{\text{sum of nuclei in 4 corner squares}}{4} \times 2\ (\text{dilution factor}) \times 10^4$$

Divide by 1000 to convert to nuclei/µL for loading calculations.
:::
